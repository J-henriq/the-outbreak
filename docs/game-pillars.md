# Game Pillars

## Pillar 1: Earned Power

Power is not handed out.

- Better gear requires stronger materials
- Better materials come from harder zones
- Harder zones require stronger builds and teamwork
- Endgame gear must be crafted and earned through boss kills, not just bought

This is the loop that makes progression feel valuable.

## Pillar 2: Mechanical Mastery

The game should reward understanding, not button spam.

- Bosses have readable mechanics
- Abilities interact with each other
- Weapon choices matter
- Positioning matters
- Team composition matters
- Resource timing matters

A casual player can enjoy it. A serious player can master it.

## Pillar 3: Long-Term Character Ownership

The player should feel attached to their character.

- Custom race
- Custom class
- Custom talents
- Visible gear progression
- Skill progression from low level to high mastery
- Playstyle identity

When people see another player, they should instantly know what kind of build that player is running.

## Pillar 4: A World Worth Living In

The world is not just combat arenas.

- Forests for woodcutting
- Rivers and coasts for fishing
- Mines for ore
- Settlements for crafting and trade
- Ruins and shrines for prayer and relic systems
- Outlaw camps and locked routes for thieving
- Traversal routes and risky shortcuts for agility

The world must feel economically and mechanically alive.

## Pillar 5: Premium Presentation

The game must look expensive.

- Stylized premium 3D
- High-quality VFX
- Strong silhouettes
- Memorable boss arenas
- Clean cinematic UI
- Rich lighting
- Atmosphere and sound design

The first 30 seconds need to sell value.

## Monetizable Appeal

If people are going to say they would pay for this, the game needs:

- Great first impression
- Deep progression
- Gear that looks aspirational
- Combat that feels sharp
- Replayable PvM
- Meaningful co-op
- Strong world fantasy

People pay for games when they feel the game has identity, depth, polish, and prestige. Ashenwild needs all four.
